How to tell them apart

parser. The shared parser reads every date month first, and every tenant on file writes the day first, so day and month swap. The bodies say it plainly: the booked date and the date on the invite are the same two numbers the other way round. It comes in through the app, by email and through booking-v2’s form. The queued fix is for this one.

hint. Booking-v3’s form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type what the hint tells them, month first, and the form reads the field the tenant’s way, day first. The symptom is the same swap, but the code at fault is the form, not the parser: fixing the parser leaves it as it is. It only comes in through booking-v3’s form.

One-day moves. A few reports the desk read as a date moving back by one day, or two people seeing two days for the same meeting (“Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday.”). The desk files them by tenant, so they are counted under parser and listed there. A month-first parser swaps day and month and does not move a date by one day, so the parser ticket names them: if they outlast the parser fix, they are a time-zone problem of their own.

Merging on the subject line cannot see any of this: the same subject lines come in under different problems, and nobody reads the body. Decision 0001 needs a new record that replaces it: merge on cause (tenant, way in, what the body shows), not on subject.
